## Research Findings
- Existing evidence shows hidden states predict eventual episode failure, but prediction alone does not establish a causal mechanism.
- The detailed pilot used task-held-out probes and fold-local preprocessing, which limits direct task leakage but does not remove progress, visual-state, behavior-policy, or termination confounding.
- VLM representation divergence preceded action-expert and action-output divergence in referenceable pilot failures; this temporal ordering is suggestive but not causal.
- Raw per-step thresholds have high any-step false-alarm rates, so deployment-grade warning requires temporal modelling and calibration.
- The complete experiment tree is 41 GB: 39 GB of immutable runs, 1.3 GB of project-local checkpoints/assets, and about 116 MB of reports.
- SmolVLA-specific material also exists inside LingBot as an 11 GB Conda environment, a 157 MB LeRobot checkout, two small obsolete virtual environments (13 MB and 14 MB), and a dependency log.
- A second Conda environment exists at `/home/zhongzhengyang/miniconda3/envs/smolvla-libero`, but it lacks pandas and is not suitable for the completed offline analysis. The working Python is the standalone project's `local/lingbot-conda-env-archived/bin/python`.
- The experiment tree includes the canonical 400-episode benchmark (19 GB), four 4–4.5 GB perturbation runs, the 3.6 GB clean Spatial pilot, smoke runs, source, tests, reports, and project-local checkpoint/assets.
- The 400-episode benchmark succeeded on 255/400 episodes (63.75%; 95% CI 59.0–68.5%) with strong suite heterogeneity: Goal 83%, Object 67%, Spatial 61%, and LIBERO-10 44%.
- On the 400-episode benchmark, action-expert hidden states are the strongest mid-trajectory warning feature: AUPRC 0.526 at step 0, 0.603 through step 50, 0.844 through step 100, and 0.968 retrospectively over the full trajectory. VLM final states reach 0.442, 0.508, 0.770, and 0.905 respectively. The task-grouped majority baseline is about 0.328.
- Sampled action uncertainty is weak on the broad benchmark (AUPRC 0.352 through step 100 and 0.638 full trajectory), despite slightly higher descriptive uncertainty in failures.
- The paired perturbation study provides valid causal effects for the specific interventions and nine-task subset: wrist masking reduced success by 65.6 percentage points to zero; mild main-camera blur reduced it by 25.6 points; the paraphrase effect was -2.2 points with a confidence interval crossing zero.
- Condition-aware probes partly recognize perturbation identity, especially the wrist mask. High discrimination in that study is not evidence of a condition-invariant latent failure mechanism.
- No human failure onset or subtype labels exist. Current outcomes are success versus timeout, and normalized-progress bins are not physical manipulation phases.
- Existing activations are pooled vectors from selected VLM/action-expert layers. Token boundaries are unavailable, so language-token versus image-token representation claims cannot be made from current artifacts.
- The benchmark already provides episode/task/instruction/seed/initial-state metadata, 89,064 step records, 286,640 probe-prediction rows, 1,305 episode/layer divergence rows, and 143,320 activation-summary rows. These support new offline robustness and confounding analyses without new simulation.
- The benchmark step table retains robot/eef/gripper state, predicted action chunks, executed actions, action dynamics, timing, uncertainty references, and activation references. It does not retain explicit modality-token boundaries or causal intervention assignments.
- The independently installed Miniconda environment at `/home/zhongzhengyang/miniconda3/envs/smolvla-libero` lacks pandas and is not the completed experiment runtime. The archived LingBot-local environment remains functional after relocation and successfully reads all result tables.
- A new within-task confound audit changes the interpretation of step-0 performance. At step 0, task identity explains about 89.5% of action-expert score variance and 80.1% of VLM score variance; within-task pairwise AUROC is only 0.543 and 0.510. The modest step-0 headline AUPRC therefore appears dominated by task/difficulty encoding rather than episode-specific foreknowledge.
- By step 100, within-task pairwise AUROC rises to 0.878 for the action expert and 0.846 for the VLM, while task-attributable score variance falls to 52.5% and 58.9%. This is much stronger evidence that the representations acquire trajectory-specific failure information during interaction.
- Warning-score dynamics are outcome-specific: from step 0 through 100, action-expert risk decreases by 0.074 on average for successes and increases by 0.121 for failures; VLM risk decreases by 0.024 for successes and increases by 0.170 for failures. Action-uncertainty risk rises by essentially the same amount for both outcomes (~0.025), explaining its poor discriminative value.
- Robot-state scores remain overwhelmingly task-structured even at step 100 (96.6% task-attributable variance), whereas hidden-state scores become substantially less task-dominated. This suggests the mid-trajectory signal is not reducible to the current low-dimensional robot state alone.
- Step-100 action-expert discrimination remains strong across suites by AUROC: LIBERO-10 0.810, Goal 0.878, Object 0.973, and Spatial 0.918. VLM AUROC is 0.722, 0.756, 0.910, and 0.871 respectively. This supports broad but non-uniform suite transfer and motivates a stricter leave-one-suite-out test.
- The new audit is deterministic: rerunning it reproduced all three derived CSVs byte-for-byte by SHA-256.
- The Phase 1 offline robustness gate used 2,000 deterministic episode and whole-task cluster bootstrap draws. At step 100, action-expert AUPRC is 0.844 (episode CI 0.785–0.895; task-cluster CI 0.736–0.927) and within-task AUROC is 0.878 (task-cluster CI 0.822–0.930). VLM AUPRC is 0.770 (episode CI 0.699–0.835; task-cluster CI 0.678–0.867) and within-task AUROC is 0.846 (task-cluster CI 0.772–0.912).
- A richer baseline containing the current state/action features plus rolling mean, standard deviation, and change over the latest five activation-sampled steps reaches only 0.517 AUPRC and 0.652 within-task AUROC at step 100. It does not explain away the hidden-state advantage.
- Leave-one-suite-out step-100 macro AUPRC is 0.835 for the action expert and 0.785 for the VLM, compared with 0.534 for the richer history baseline. Action-expert AUROC remains 0.832–0.964 in each held-out suite.
- Task-held-out action-expert probes transfer in both directions between clean and main-camera blur (step-100 AUROC 0.867/0.727) and between clean and instruction paraphrase (0.728/0.750). VLM transfer is strong for blur (0.752/0.738) but weaker for paraphrases (0.608/0.597).
- Clean probes score the all-failure wrist-mask condition as high risk at step 100 (mean 0.924 action expert; 0.941 VLM), but outcome-ranking metrics are undefined. Wrist-mask-to-clean fitting is impossible because wrist-mask training labels contain only failures; this direction is explicitly marked non-estimable rather than treated as evidence.
- Phase 1 therefore supports a robust step-100 predictive signal, especially in the action expert, while rejecting any claim that the current evidence proves a universal condition-invariant failure direction or causal hidden-state mechanism.

## Blind-Spot Audit (in progress)

- The rollout policy uses `n_action_steps=50`: one model inference fills a 50-action queue, and subsequent environment steps execute queued actions without a new model forward until the queue is empty.
- Activation observations are saved every five environment steps, but activations are not captured during the rollout. After the episode, `_save_activations` resets the policy and calls `predict_action_chunk` separately on every saved observation.
- `predict_action_chunk` samples fresh Gaussian flow-matching noise unless noise is supplied. The archived activation at a state is therefore a one-sample stochastic post-hoc re-query, not necessarily the hidden state of the action chunk that generated the trajectory.
- Action-expert hooks fire during iterative flow-matching denoising. The recorder averages each layer over all action-token positions and then averages repeated hook invocations. This summary can encode generated action-plan geometry and denoising dynamics; it is not yet a localized scene-understanding representation.
- Because recorded `predicted_action_chunk` is the shrinking queue from the original rollout while hidden activations come from a fresh post-hoc 50-step prediction, the current policy-output baseline is not aligned to the action-expert activation it is intended to control.
- These facts preserve the interpretation that post-hoc representations predict outcome from recorded observations, but materially weaken any claim that the archived vectors were on the causal path of the original actions.
- Runtime latency confirms the action-queue boundary: median policy latency is 127.2 ms when `env_step % 50 == 0` versus roughly 1.0–1.2 ms at non-boundary steps, a 120.6× ratio.
- The existing through-step-100 metric has a risk-set flaw: 81/255 successful episodes (31.8% of successes; 20.3% of all episodes) have already terminated by step 100, whereas no failed episode has terminated. It therefore compares some known-completed successes against ongoing failures.
- A strict step-100 landmark restricted to the 319 still-active episodes remains predictive: action-expert AUPRC/AUROC is 0.851/0.854 and VLM is 0.829/0.859. Termination leakage inflates or changes the estimand but does not fully explain the signal.
- The action-expert arrays have shape 720 and pool source tensors of shape `[1, 50, 720]` across all 50 ordered action tokens. Each stored vector also averages 10 hook invocations across the flow-matching denoising process. VLM vectors pool 177 prefix tokens into 960 dimensions with one invocation.
- The prior policy baseline discards action order. A task-held-out probe on the complete ordered 50×7 action chunk at true replanning boundaries reaches step-100 AUPRC 0.791, AUROC 0.809, and within-task AUROC 0.805 among active episodes. This closes most of the gap to the action-expert values 0.851/0.854/0.841 and makes action-plan recognition a leading explanation.
- The official LeRobot SmolVLA documentation describes language, multiple cameras, and sensorimotor state as contextual inputs conditioning an action expert that generates an action chunk. This supports treating the action-expert representation as a joint plan-generation state, not as a pure semantic scene representation.
- Recent primary VLA work identifies the complementary language-grounding risk: LIBERO-CF reports visually driven counterfactual failures in which policies execute frequent training behaviors despite alternative language instructions, while ICBench reports "linguistic blindness" under instructions that contradict the scene. The project must test both visual dominance and language/template dominance rather than presuppose one.
- AC-VLA independently identifies two threats that align unusually closely with this audit: trajectory overfitting to holistic action patterns rather than compositional sub-skill semantics, and a wrist-texture perceptual shortcut that displaces global spatial grounding. This makes action-plan and camera-shortcut controls central rather than optional.
- LIBERO-Para reports 22–52 percentage-point degradation across seven VLA configurations under controlled paraphrases, driven mainly by object-level lexical substitutions, with most failures attributed to planning-level trajectory divergence. The project's one benign paraphrase per instruction is therefore too weak to establish language understanding.
- ProbeAct demonstrates a useful alternative interpretation of intermediate VLA features: lightweight probes can decode task-relevant object positions, while failure detection can be handled by an explicit kinematic state machine. The current project should compare semantic/geometric decodability with outcome decodability rather than treating a failure probe as the only lens.
- Ordinary probe accuracy is not evidence that the representation itself contains the intended concept. Hewitt and Liang's control-task/selectivity framework shows that a capable probe can fit arbitrary mappings. Conditional probing gives the directly relevant test here: compare a grouped baseline containing task, robot state, time/queue position, and the full ordered action chunk against that same baseline plus the hidden state. Incremental held-out log loss or Brier score is more probative than raw hidden-state AUPRC alone.
- Activation patching needs an explicit causal abstraction, not only an intervention that changes behavior. Causal Abstraction, Distributed Alignment Search, and Interchange Intervention Training formalize tests of hypothesized causal variables in distributed representations. AtP* can screen sites efficiently but may miss effects, so any claimed mechanism still requires exact matched-state intervention tests.
- A broad survival/landmark-analysis web search returned an overlong truncated result and no usable source. This is a search/tool failure, not evidence; narrower primary-source queries are required before selecting the time-to-event estimand.
- The current task-held-out split tests whether a probe transfers across tasks on trajectories produced by a policy that was itself trained or fine-tuned on those LIBERO task families. It does **not** test policy-level task generalization, unseen composition, unseen layout, or distance from the training demonstrations.
- LIBERO-PRO was created specifically because standard LIBERO scores can be inflated by memorized action sequences and layouts. It perturbs objects, initial positions, instruction semantics/tasks, and environments; its reported standard-to-generalized collapse, including persistent actions under corrupted instructions, makes a genuine unseen-layout/task counterfactual essential for this project.
- Official LIBERO-PRO code exposes separable object, position, semantic-language, task, and environment evaluations. This provides a low-engineering-cost route to distinguish familiar-trajectory execution from grounded control before moving to a larger VLA.
- Recent action-chunk work (SV-VLA and VLA-Corrector) treats long chunks as an explicit open-loop blind spot: stale plans accumulate error between observations, motivating continuous verification or event-triggered replanning. SmolVLA's `n_action_steps=50` is therefore both a potential source of failures and a confound for interpreting a hidden-state warning as model understanding.
- Code-level confirmation: `_policy_features` summarizes a 50×7 chunk with per-action-dimension mean, standard deviation, minimum, and maximum. It discards which motion occurs at which future timestep. The much stronger full ordered-chunk result is therefore a correction of an underpowered control, not merely an extra baseline.
- All 89,064 archived `object_state` values are null. Every `goal_state` is only `{'phase_evidence': 'normalized_progress_fallback'}`, and the apparently semantic `task_phase` labels are deterministic normalized-time bins. There is currently no privileged evidence that an object was approached, grasped, transported, or placed.
- Consequently, phase-conditioned results can still be trajectory-clock effects. Phase names must not be presented as physical event semantics until reconstructed from simulator object poses, contacts, gripper state, and task predicates or labeled from observation prefixes without outcome hindsight.
- The canonical config explicitly sets `save_environment_state: true`, but no object/environment state was actually populated in the step table. This is an instrumentation-contract failure: the artifacts cannot currently restore matched physical states, define event-grounded phases, or estimate progress independently of time.
- The local `checkpoints/libero_datasets` directory contains no accessible training demonstrations (approximately 4 KB directory metadata only). Existing evaluation trajectories can test trajectory clustering, but a direct nearest-neighbor training-demonstration memorization claim is not estimable from the present artifacts.
- The trajectory-confound audit must use exact replanning landmarks and identical active-episode/task-held-out folds. Ordered executed-action prefix and ordered current plan should be evaluated separately and jointly; hidden-state incremental value should use fold-local blockwise transforms so concatenation does not let a shared PCA silently discard one modality.
- A reproducible five-fold task-grouped exact-landmark audit confirms that trajectory/plan context explains a substantial fraction, but not all, of step-100 discrimination. Among 319 active episodes, current robot state + ordered actions through step 99 + the full step-100 plan reaches AUPRC/AUROC/within-task AUROC 0.803/0.821/0.803 after nested task-grouped regularization selection.
- Adding post-hoc hidden state to that behavioral context improves step-100 results materially: action expert reaches AUPRC/AUROC/within-task AUROC 0.882/0.891/0.843 and VLM reaches 0.883/0.891/0.863. Log loss falls from 0.532 to 0.435/0.434 and Brier score from 0.177 to 0.137/0.141. Thus ordered behavior does not fully explain the signal.
- The remaining hidden-state increment is still not synonymous with semantic understanding. The behavioral baseline has no pixels or object state, while both hidden pathways are recomputed from the current images and a fresh stochastic plan. Residual value could reflect visual progress, object displacement, contact appearance, or the unrecorded re-query action plan rather than task semantics.
- Untuned concatenated probes were initially poorly calibrated. This was resolved by selecting logistic regularization from five values inside nested task-grouped folds, with scaling and blockwise PCA refitted inside every inner split. Only the nested results above should be used.
- All 400 episodes retain per-step uint8 RGB observations for two 256×256 cameras, plus robot/policy state and executed actions. Although privileged environment state is missing, these frames support prefix-only failure annotation and a cheap low-resolution pixel/frame-change control for generic visual progress.
- A pixel control is the next discriminating offline test: if adding downsampled current images or change from the initial image eliminates the hidden-state increment, the representation is mostly a visual progress monitor; if hidden state still adds value, stronger semantic/geometric targets and counterfactuals become justified.
- The low-resolution pixel audit reveals visible initial-condition difficulty. At step 0, current two-camera pixels reach AUPRC/AUROC/within-task AUROC 0.532/0.632/0.607, outperforming action expert (0.474/0.597/0.535) and VLM (0.466/0.617/0.517) under the nested five-fold task-grouped protocol. Some apparent foreknowledge is therefore recoverable directly from initial layout/appearance.
- At step 100, current low-resolution pixels are predictive but weaker than behavioral context or hidden state: AUPRC/AUROC/within-task AUROC 0.721/0.774/0.731. Absolute pixel change from the initial frame is much weaker at 0.512/0.612/0.699. Generic visual progress contributes, but does not explain the full hidden-state signal.
- High-dimensional visual+behavioral concatenations transferred and calibrated poorly across held-out tasks despite nested scalar regularization; their global proper-score comparisons are not conclusive. A better next control is privileged geometric state or a frozen visual embedding with separately regularized/gated blocks, not more raw-pixel probe capacity.
- The official `lerobot/smolvla_libero` model card links its training data directly to `lerobot/libero`. That dataset has one training split with roughly 273k frames, 1,690 episodes, and task indices 0–39. The canonical evaluation also uses the same 40 LIBERO task identities. Thus current task holdout isolates probe fitting only; the policy is being evaluated in-domain with respect to task identity.
- Official SmolVLA guidance explicitly recommends repeated demonstrations for each task variation and notes that adequate coverage of positions improves performance. This is sensible engineering but underscores that success on sampled familiar variations cannot establish systematic compositional grounding.
- The June 2026 primary study *What Are We Actually Benchmarking in Robot Manipulation?* independently classifies LIBERO as shortcut-solvable: a 0.09B probe with no language encoder and no large-scale robotics pretraining performs at or near reported state of the art. It also highlights statistical-significance, fixed-test/distribution overfitting, and data-source-dependence failures.
- Official LIBERO evaluation loads a fixed set of task initial states by index. The current benchmark uses only indices 0–9 per task. Even without proving exact demonstration-state overlap, repeated reuse of this narrow fixed evaluation support permits sample-level adaptation and makes initial-state counterfactuals mandatory.
- LIBERO was originally designed for lifelong knowledge transfer, with LIBERO-90 versus LIBERO-10 playing distinct training/downstream roles. A checkpoint fine-tuned jointly on the 40 standard evaluation tasks changes the scientific estimand from unseen-task transfer to within-task imitation robustness.
- Drop-Then-Recovery finds that language backbones are much more removable/recoverable than vision and action pathways on standard robot benchmarks; on LIBERO, an OpenVLA-OFT variant retaining only two language blocks can recover baseline-level performance. Standard task success therefore applies little pressure to deep linguistic grounding.
- Anchor-Align identifies a different but compatible mechanism: behavior-cloning fine-tuning can overwrite pretrained vision-language representations, while web-data co-training does not guarantee language-action alignment because the two losses act on different observations. A strong pretrained language model is not the same as a language-grounded policy.
- ICBench and LIBERO-CF show visual priors overriding contradictory language, whereas LIBERO-Para and multilingual evaluation show severe sensitivity to surface form. These are not contradictory: a policy can ignore instruction meaning when the scene identifies a familiar template yet fail when wording changes enough to disrupt task lookup.
- Multilingual step-wise analysis reports non-uniform language dependence across control time. The correct unit is therefore a decision/event-conditioned language effect, especially at object selection, reorientation, and placement decisions—not one global paraphrase success delta.
- The project's original headline is no longer a sufficient novelty claim. *What Frozen VLAs Already Know About Success* already probes Monte-Carlo success from frozen OpenVLA, Pi0.5, DINOv2, and CLIP features on LIBERO, uses same-task/same-timestep matching, and improves behavior by selecting among sampled action prefixes.
- Hide-and-Seek already addresses uniform propagation of trajectory labels by learning temporally localized failure signals with contrastive objectives, while SAFE covers multitask conformal failure monitoring. A better AUPRC alone would not establish a research gap.
- Event-Grounded Sparse Autoencoders already connect VLA features to behavioral keyframes and closed-loop interventions, and report that highly predictive features can have weak causal effects. This directly warns against equating probe ranking with mechanism ranking.
- The defensible open gap is a **causal decomposition of value-like signal**: determine whether a representation tracks (a) semantic task state, (b) the policy's sampled action plan, (c) generic visual/kinematic progress, or (d) policy-conditioned recoverability under alternative continuations. Existing local artifacts conflate all four.
- A single realized binary outcome is a noisy label for a stochastic flow policy. The more principled target at state `s_t` is policy-conditioned recoverability `V^pi(s_t, i) = P(success | s_t, instruction i, policy pi)`, estimated by restoring the same simulator state and branching multiple continuations with controlled diffusion noise.
- The closest paper's overlap is direct: it reports SmolVLA task-split value-like probing at `R^2=0.5257`, so merely adding the SmolVLA backbone or task-held-out regression is not novel. It also includes goal swaps and a simulator-backed best-of-16 candidate-prefix selector.
- Its target assigns discounted outcome along one completed trajectory and explicitly disclaims Bellman consistency. Its same-task/same-timestep matching controls task and time, but not ordered action plan, current pixels/geometry, stochastic noise, or multiple possible futures from the identical physical state.
- Its candidate selector restores simulator snapshots and evaluates short prefixes, but uses simulator reward/success plus a probe to choose actions; it does not identify whether the hidden representation itself estimates the success distribution under controlled alternative continuations.
- This sharpens the candidate contribution: **Counterfactual Recoverability Decomposition (CRD)**. At a restored state, estimate recoverability across common-random-number continuation branches; then measure conditional usable information and causal interventions while independently varying instruction semantics, scene semantics, action chunk, and flow-noise seed.
- In the closest paper's goal swaps, VLM features change but the original instruction receives a higher score in only 53.7% of pairs for one probe, with the mean direction reversing across seeds/layers/tasks. “Language affects the representation” therefore does not imply correct goal-conditioned valuation.
- Event-grounded SAE results show that action-expert feature zeroing can drive near-total policy collapse for many ranking schemes, including weak/random alternatives. A large success drop under destructive ablation is not feature-specific causal evidence; controller fidelity, matched-source patching, dose response, and semantic direction are required.
- The existing `docs/causal_research_program.md` anticipated predictive-versus-causal distinctions but predates the action-queue/post-hoc mismatch, raw trajectory/pixel controls, direct novelty collision, and recoverability estimand. Later phases must be reordered rather than simply appending more annotation and perturbations.
- A high-information two-task smoke pair exists in the benchmark's shared LIBERO-Goal scene: task 3, “open the top drawer and put the bowl inside” (6/10 successes), and task 4, “put the bowl on top of the cabinet” (10/10). They share the bowl but require incompatible destination and subskill structure, making them suitable for cross-goal plan faithfulness if a common physical-state/multi-predicate evaluator is implemented.
- The complete official `lerobot/libero` repository is approximately 1.94 GB and separates tabular data from videos. Downloading only data/meta shards should make training-trajectory action/state nearest-neighbor analysis feasible without the image corpus, subject to explicit approval before external download.

## Cross-Architecture Overlook-Discovery Audit

- The official `openpi` repository now exposes π0.5 base, DROID, and LIBERO checkpoints plus both JAX and PyTorch inference paths. The supported π0.5 implementation uses a flow-matching action head, so controlled proposal-noise and action-chunk branching are technically plausible without making SmolVLA-specific assumptions.
- The official GR00T repository has advanced beyond N1.5 to N1.7 and provides a LIBERO checkpoint. For a durable paper, the baseline should be version-pinned; N1.5 remains scientifically informative because it freezes its Eagle VLM, cross-attends those embeddings from a flow-matching DiT, and adds future-latent alignment, whereas later releases change the backbone.
- π0.5, GR00T, and SmolVLA differ in pretraining corpus, semantic supervision, VLM update rules, action horizon, and embodiment adapters. Comparing their success or latent probes directly would confound architecture with training and system design.
- Cross-model methodology must therefore define targets in environment/effect space—physical state, goal predicate, induced state transition, plan-conditioned outcome, and policy recoverability—while treating each model's hidden sites as model-local measurements.
- SVA (*Look Before You Leap*, July 2026) already performs simulator tree search over frozen-VLA proposals, distills empirical returns into an action-value model, and selects candidates at deployment. “Sample several chunks and learn a Q evaluator” is therefore no longer a defensible primary novelty claim.
- Reflective VLA already identifies hidden embodiment factors such as camera geometry, calibration, and actuation bias using observation–action–consequence triplets. Simply adding causal transition history to a VLA also overlaps published work.
- Deployment-time reliability work already covers unsupervised progress/consistency monitoring, training-demonstration influence attribution, and behavior-sequence success estimation. The method should use these as baselines rather than rebrand them.
- The sharper unresolved target is **policy-relative competence**: from an identical state and goal, distinguish difficulty shared by every policy from model-specific inability and proposal-specific bad luck. Current value/action-selection work generally evaluates one policy at a time and optimizes it; it does not use several frozen VLA families as counterfactual controls for causal failure attribution.
- “Competence” is an established robotics concept, and recent work provides sample-efficient sequential tests for aggregate policy comparison. The novelty cannot be generic competence estimation or a more efficient leaderboard comparison.
- Recent foresight-value work estimates downstream success from terminal subtask states for one VLA/residual policy. This overlaps state-value and handoff-quality estimation but still does not factor a matched state into shared environment hardness versus policy-family-specific recoverability.
- A careful novelty statement should be: a **statewise cross-policy causal matrix** plus factor-interaction discovery and internal self-specificity tests—not the individual ingredients of value estimation, competence awareness, policy comparison, or branching.
- Diagnostic benchmark space is crowded: RADAR varies real-world dynamics and spatial/physical factors; ForesightSafety-VLA spans scene, language, and visual safety axes across embodiments; LIBERO-PRO/Plus cover several deployment shifts. A new list of perturbations is not enough.
- ROBOGATE already applies broad sampling followed by adaptive failure-boundary sampling in a parameterized simulator. Boundary-focused testing alone is also not novel.
- The proposed discovery unit should instead be a **matched-state policy × causal-factor interaction**. Main effects answer which perturbations are generally hard; interactions reveal overlooked dependencies such as “language matters only for π0.5 at object-selection events under novel position” or “GR00T fails only when calibration shift and long action horizon coincide.”
- The method must confirm discovered interactions with a fresh held-out counterfactual intervention and an environment-level effect, rather than interpreting a flexible risk-model interaction as causal evidence.
- Official LIBERO checkpoints make a common simulator comparison feasible: π0.5-LIBERO reports 96.85% mean standard-LIBERO success and GR00T N1.7-LIBERO reports roughly 97.0%. This benchmark is too saturated to be the primary scientific endpoint; it is suitable for instrumentation parity and state-bank construction.
- Native action horizons differ materially: official π0.5-LIBERO is configured for 10 steps, GR00T N1.7 supports a maximum of 16, and the current SmolVLA runtime executes 50. Replanning horizon is therefore both a causal factor and a system-level confound.
- Cross-policy evaluation needs two horizon conditions: each policy's native horizon (ecological system performance) and a common executed prefix/replanning cadence when technically valid (controller-normalized comparison). Neither should be mislabeled as a pure architecture effect.
- *How VLAs Fail Differently* already compares black-box action-monitoring signatures across VQ-BeT, Diffusion Policy, and ACT. “Architecture-specific failure signatures” is therefore an occupied headline; the new method must go beyond trajectory statistics to matched-state causal recoverability and shared-versus-policy-specific variance.
- *Same Weights, Different Robot* shows that action normalization metadata and controller conventions are part of the executable policy and can collapse LIBERO replay success even with identical weights. Every policy adapter must emit an executable-policy certificate covering checkpoint, processor, normalization statistics, action representation, horizon, and controller mapping.
- EQRL already learns difficulty-aware denoising and action-chunk schedules. The proposed work may manipulate horizon as a diagnostic factor but should not claim adaptive query scheduling as the contribution.
- BOKBO shows that K-sample disagreement can track the injected noise scale rather than actual violation probability. Native stochastic seeds, externally perturbed actions, and candidate diversity must be logged and analyzed as distinct mechanisms; raw disagreement is not an uncertainty ground truth.
- An exact-name search found no robotics use of “Cross-Policy Recoverability Decomposition” as of 2026-07-27, but novelty must be established from method substance rather than naming.
- A July 2026 locomotion paper already separates global adapter gain, same-state counterfactual headroom, deployment gain, and state-allocation gain. Same-state branching or “headroom” alone is not new; the defensible scope is multi-VLA shared-hardness/policy-residual decomposition plus causal factor-interaction discovery.
- A Rasch/IRT-style policy-ability minus state-difficulty model is a useful conceptual null, but recent AI-evaluation simulations warn that item-level IRT estimates can be unreliable with few, clustered systems. With only SmolVLA, π0.5, and GR00T, do not claim a universal latent ability scale.
- Use direct paired statewise log-odds contrasts plus a partially pooled cross-classified binomial model, validate estimator recovery in synthetic simulations, and describe the across-policy component as empirical common-mode difficulty. Add a simple non-language or behavior-cloning control policy if feasible to improve triangulation, not merely model count.
- Candidate method: **Cross-Policy Recoverability Decomposition (CPRD)**. For policy `m`, restored physical state `s`, goal `g`, factor setting `z`, native proposal seed `k`, and continuation seed `r`, observe `Y[m,s,g,z,k,r]`. Estimate `V_m(s,g,z)=E_{k,r}[Y]` and plan deviation `Q_m(s,g,z,k)-V_m(s,g,z)`.
- Fit a simulation-calibrated cross-classified binomial model whose terms separate state common mode, global policy baseline, known factor main effects, policy×state residual, policy×factor interactions, factor×factor interactions, proposal deviation, and continuation noise. Report direct paired policy contrasts alongside the model so inference does not depend on one factorization.
- Define a policy-state blind spot as a reproducible policy×state or policy×factor residual after executable-policy metadata, privileged geometry, goal predicate, training-retrieval distance, and proposal effect are controlled. A high failure rate alone is not a blind spot.
- Do not compare raw action chunks across architectures. Execute a standardized short prefix from a cloned state and map it to an environment-effect vector: end-effector/object displacement, contact and attachment transitions, target/receptacle distances, predicate changes, collision/force, and elapsed control time. Compare native and equalized replanning tracks separately.
- Test internal **self-specificity** rather than generic decodability. For each model-local hidden state, measure incremental proper-score value for (a) common-mode state difficulty, (b) that policy's statewise competence residual, (c) that sampled plan's deviation, and (d) other policies' matched-state outcomes. A representation that predicts every policy equally is a scene-difficulty monitor; preferential own-policy prediction supports introspective competence.
- Candidate self-specificity contrast: own-policy conditional log-score improvement minus mean other-policy improvement at the same states. Use grouped cross-fitting, complete observable/effect baselines, and paired uncertainty; do not align latent dimensions or patch activations between architectures.
- Overlook-discovery loop: (1) screen executable semantic, visual, physical, history, and controller factors with a cost-aware fractional design; (2) fit sparse hierarchical interaction terms; (3) rank candidate cells by posterior policy disagreement, unexplained residual, interaction uncertainty, and cost; (4) execute the most informative counterfactual; (5) accept a factor only after a fresh held-out task/state-family paired confirmation with the predicted direction.
- LLM/VLM suggestions may nominate factors but cannot constitute evidence. Every accepted factor must map to a typed simulator/controller variable, preserve all non-target variables, and pass manipulation/fidelity checks.
- Use two cross-model tracks. The ecological track uses official version-pinned LIBERO checkpoints and native processors/horizons, supporting system-level claims. The controlled post-training track uses the same task data, cameras, action convention, and evaluation state bank as far as possible, but still must not claim a pure architecture effect because pretraining differs.
- Proposed cross-policy baseline set: SmolVLA for method development, official π0.5-LIBERO, and version-pinned GR00T N1.7-LIBERO as current primary systems; optionally add GR00T N1.5 to isolate the frozen-Eagle/FLARE design and a small no-language policy as a shortcut control.
- Cost ladder: first validate the policy-adapter and effect-space contract without rollout expansion; then run a SmolVLA-only restored-state pilot; then perform short-prefix screening across the three policies; reserve full `K×M` continuations for a small set of high-disagreement cells and independent confirmations. Every simulation expansion remains approval-gated.
- Novelty boundary after the current search: CPRD should not claim value probing, action selection, same-state branching, competence estimation, action-consequence memory, architecture-specific failure signatures, adaptive horizon scheduling, perturbation benchmarking, or failure-boundary sampling individually. Its candidate contribution is their missing identification layer: state-matched multi-policy decomposition, internal self-specificity, effect-space normalization, and sequential causal interaction discovery with independent confirmation.

## Technical Decisions
| Decision | Rationale |
|----------|-----------|
| Separate descriptive, predictive, mechanistic, and causal claims | Each requires different evidence and prevents overclaiming. |
| Use matched counterfactual interventions and mediation analysis | These directly test modality contribution and the VLM→action pathway. |
| Keep task/episode groups isolated across all fitted transforms | Avoid leakage from correlated trajectory samples. |
| Move the complete 41 GB experiment tree and LingBot-local SmolVLA support directories | This satisfies physical separation while preserving raw evidence. |
| Keep the already-external Miniconda environment in place | It is outside LingBot and can be referenced as a dependency. |
| Prioritize exact action-forward alignment and restorable physical-state instrumentation before annotation or another rollout | Archived activations did not generate the recorded actions, and the current recorder omits the geometry/predicates needed to distinguish state recoverability from plan quality. |
| Use branched recoverability plus matched bidirectional patching as the central mechanistic test | Generic prediction or destructive activation ablation cannot distinguish semantic state, progress, sampled-plan quality, and controller damage. |
| Reframe step-0 probe results as a task-confounding diagnostic | Within-task ranking is near chance despite above-baseline global AUPRC. |
| Focus the next mechanistic window around steps 50–100 | This is where within-task hidden-state separation emerges and uncertainty fails to track outcome-specific change. |
| Use a three-stage cost gate | Forward-only alignment and invariance must precede a two-task branched CRD smoke study; actual OOD composition and stronger architectures follow only after a causal signal survives. |
| Replace the annotation-first phase with the CRD measurement gate | The hidden-state advantage survives estimable offline controls, but exact forward/noise/chunk alignment and physical-state restoration are logically prior to event labels or mechanistic interpretation. |
| Separate state value `V` from plan-conditioned value `Q` | Repeated branches from an identical state are needed to determine whether a feature represents recoverability or only the quality of one stochastic action sample. |
| Require cross-goal directionality, rescue/induction, and hook fidelity | Representation change or controller collapse is insufficient evidence of grounded language or a localized causal mechanism. |

## Phase 2 Instrumentation Audit

- The current activation path is scientifically misaligned by construction: rollout actions are produced with `policy.select_action`, while activations are collected only after the episode by resetting the policy and calling a fresh stochastic `predict_action_chunk` on saved observations.
- `ActivationCapture` currently averages every non-feature dimension and `_save_activations` then averages repeated hook invocations. This collapses batch/token or action-position structure and all flow-denoising calls into one vector, so modality spans, action positions, and denoising dynamics cannot be recovered.
- The current step schema has no action-query ID, flow-noise seed, queue age, source chunk index, or exact action-producing activation reference. Those fields are required to connect one generated chunk to the actions executed from its queue.
- `runtime.execute` explicitly writes `object_state=None` and time-derived goal/phase fallbacks even when `save_environment_state` is enabled. Restorable simulator state and privileged geometry require a new adapter contract rather than a report-layer fix.
- Phase 2 should instrument the existing `select_action`/queue boundary and preserve structured records before adding any latent-space analysis. Otherwise modality or self-knowledge conclusions would still concern a counterfactual re-query rather than the policy decision that drove the robot.
- Code-level architecture: `VLAFlowMatching.sample_actions` constructs the image/language/state prefix once, fills a per-layer KV cache, and then performs ten action-expert denoising calls. The action expert consumes noisy action-time tokens as queries and the cached prefix as keys/values. This makes denoising-time trajectories in the action expert meaningful, while the VLM prefix is computed once per sampled chunk.
- Prefix token order is deterministic in `embed_prefix`: for each camera, optional two-token image start, image embeddings, optional one-token end; then language tokens; then one state token; finally optional padding. Exact modality spans can therefore be recorded from the action-producing call rather than inferred from pooled tensors.
- The existing resolved targets generate 44 hook records for eight modules: four VLM layer records from the one prefix pass and forty action-expert layer records from ten denoising steps. Averaging those records specifically erases the flow-time axis.
- LIBERO's underlying environment wrapper exposes flattened MuJoCo state retrieval/restoration (`get_sim_state`/`set_state`-style methods around `sim.get_state().flatten()` and `set_state_from_flattened`) plus success checks and raw observation refresh. The missing restorable-state contract is implementable locally; it is not an inherent simulator limitation.
- The pinned checkpoint uses chunk size 50, ten flow steps, KV caching, cross-attention, two cameras without added image-special tokens, no prefix padding, and internal maximum state/action widths of 32. Its model weights are already present locally (about 907 MB) under `archive/full_experiment/checkpoints/smolvla_libero`; no checkpoint download is needed for Phase 2.
- With this configuration, the exact prefix span order simplifies to main-camera image tokens, wrist-camera image tokens, 48 language slots, and one state token. Valid-language masks must still be retained because padded language slots otherwise contaminate modality summaries.
- `prepare_images` follows the checkpoint's ordered image-feature mapping and includes only keys present in the batch; the LIBERO preprocessor maps `image` to camera1 and `image2` to camera2. Thus camera-token labels can be derived from the actual ordered keys, not guessed from tensor position. The declared unused camera3 is absent and `empty_cameras=0`, so it creates no prefix tokens.
- A scientifically useful storage shape is therefore explicit: VLM target arrays `[1, batch, prefix_token, hidden]` and action-expert target arrays `[10, batch, action_position, hidden]`. Language validity and modality spans should be metadata alongside these arrays; flattening either token axis would discard the proposed tests.
- Phase 2 now has a tested exact-query capture contract: explicit CPU-generated flow noise, full generated chunk, unpooled VLM/action-expert tensors, exact prefix masks/spans, per-step `x_t` and velocity, and per-token KV-cache norms. The KV cache is the concrete VLM-to-flow bridge; its modality-resolved norms and fixed-noise interventions can be studied without treating a pooled VLM vector as the mechanism.
- A restorable-state record should pair the flattened MuJoCo state with derived semantic checks at capture time. The implemented contract records every LIBERO object/site pose and joint state, each goal predicate separately, raw contact pairs, conservative robosuite bilateral-grasp status, and overall success; restoration is rejected if simulator state, predicate truth, or grasp status changes.
- The selected shared-scene pilot has useful natural contrast: LIBERO-Goal task 3 (“open the top drawer and put the bowl inside”) has 6/10 successes with both long failures and successes, while task 4 (“put the bowl on top of the cabinet”) is 10/10 and terminates around 79–87 steps. Task 3 therefore supports matched success/failure state sampling; task 4 is mainly an alternate-goal/ceiling comparison.
- Archived observation files contain both 256×256 cameras, the eight-dimensional pre-policy state, and executed actions at every realized step. They are sufficient for a no-simulation fixed-forward gate at exact steps 50/100, but cannot recover missing MuJoCo state for causal continuation.
- The first diagnostic should separate two sources of variation at each saved state: multiple flow-noise seeds under the original observation (proposal-specific variation) and fixed-noise counterfactuals for paraphrase, feasible alternate goal, main view, and wrist view (factor-specific variation). This is the local SmolVLA precursor to CPRD, not yet a recoverability experiment.
- The one-state real-checkpoint smoke passed exactness: captured versus uncaptured fixed-noise action chunks have maximum absolute difference 0; repeated query action, VLM, and action-expert tensors also differ by exactly 0. VLM activations and KV-cache token norms have zero variation across flow-noise seeds, confirming that proposal randomness enters after the cached prefix.
- At the task-3 failure trajectory's step 50, a feasible alternate goal changes the normalized action chunk far more than a paraphrase or either mean-image replacement (RMSE 0.859 versus 0.333/0.287/0.295). This is preliminary evidence that the flow policy is not wholly language-blind at that state.
- The layer-15 VLM changes are sharply localized as expected: language-span RMSE is 0.128/0.133 for alternate goal/paraphrase, main-image-span RMSE is 0.196 for main mean replacement, and wrist-image-span RMSE is 0.187 for wrist mean replacement. Cross-span changes remain much smaller but reach the state token, showing multimodal integration rather than fully isolated channels.
- A potentially important bridge signature appears within flow matching: factor-induced velocity differences grow toward later denoising calls. For the alternate goal at layer 15, velocity RMSE rises from 0.358 at `t=1.0` to 0.597 at `t=0.1`; for paraphrase it rises from 0.153 to 0.298. The action expert therefore appears to amplify conditional differences as the action sample becomes less noisy, rather than merely copying a static VLM score.
- KV-cache norm change is not a semantic-importance measure: the paraphrase produces a larger aggregate key-norm change than the alternate goal (3.009 versus 1.728) despite a much smaller action effect. Any VLM-to-flow attribution must use directional interventions or attention/causal effects, not cache magnitude alone.
- Four-state confirmation preserves exactness (zero hook-output and repeat differences) and shows zero VLM/KV variation across flow-noise seeds. The pooled proposal-action versus final action-expert distance correlation is 0.974; after centering within each state it remains 0.872, but this hides strong temporal heterogeneity.
- Proposal alignment is concentrated at the end of the flow trajectory and at step 100. At layer 15, within-state correlation rises from -0.238 at the first denoising call to 0.872 at the last; the last-call correlation is approximately 0 at environment step 50 but 0.884 at step 100. Thus a single denoising-averaged “action-expert state” conflates early noise processing with late executable-plan geometry.
- The semantic versus surface-form contrast is consistent across four states. Median action RMSE is 1.148 for a feasible alternate goal and 0.454 for a meaning-preserving paraphrase, although their final VLM RMSE is nearly the same (0.0328 versus 0.0359). Severe main/wrist mean-image replacements yield much larger VLM changes (0.135/0.126) but smaller action changes (0.326/0.334).
- This defines a candidate **direction-selective downstream gain** phenomenon: the flow/action expert amplifies some small VLM displacement directions (goal semantics) much more strongly than larger visual or lexical displacements. Raw latent distance, cache norm, or CKA cannot identify this; the relevant quantity is a directional Jacobian/causal response into executable action effects.
- SmolVLA's internal flow state is 32-dimensional per action token, but LIBERO executes only the first seven channels. The other 25 channels are not harmless: their mean denoising-velocity RMS is about 0.984 versus 1.16–1.22 for active channels, and seed-to-seed padded-channel velocity differences are comparable to active-channel differences while being unrelated to final executed-action distance.
- The model successfully drives those 25 padded outputs close to zero (final RMS about 0.0063–0.0068 versus 0.63–0.70 in the active seven). This means much of the action expert's computation and full-hidden variation can reflect **null/padding denoising work**, not robot trajectory content. Full 720-D probes and cross-model latent comparisons must be decomposed into executable-head row space, padding-head row space, and output-null space.
- The archived-chunk mismatch is now measured under exact current forwards: fresh postprocessed chunks are roughly 0.30–0.34 RMSE from the original rollout chunk at step 50 and 0.60–0.74 at step 100. Because the LIBERO environment postprocessor is identity, this is a real stochastic/query mismatch, not a coordinate-system artifact. It quantifies why archived post-hoc activations could not support action-path causality.
- Exact action-head-input capture confirms the executable-subspace hypothesis on the first state. The final expert state is 720-D; the output head has ranks 7 (executed channels), 25 (padding channels), and 32 jointly. For noise-seed differences, median energy is 8.98% in the executed row space, 65.36% in padding row space, and 74.42% in the full output row space—far above isotropic expectations of 0.97% and 4.44%.
- Input-factor differences have the opposite geometry: only 2.98–3.90% of their final expert-state energy lies in the full action-output row space, leaving 96.1–97.0% in its null space. Yet the small active projection produces meaningful executed velocity changes, while padding-output changes remain tiny. This cleanly separates **context representation magnitude** from **executable causal projection**.
- The alternate goal is especially selective: 3.19% of its expert-state delta energy lies in the executed row space and only 0.056% in padding row space; executed-head output RMSE is 1.268 versus 0.073 for padding outputs. By contrast, proposal-noise variation is dominated by padding-denoising directions. This suggests a new diagnostic pair: semantic executability quotient versus padding-denoising burden.
- These results expose why ordinary probes can appear strong without identifying a mechanism: a classifier can exploit the approximately 97% null-space context component, while behavior depends on the small row-space projection. Causal claims should therefore compare predictive information in null space against intervention/action effects in executable row space.
- Four-state action-head confirmation is consistent: proposal-noise deltas have median 9.08% executed-row energy, 60.36% padding-row energy, and 68.69% total output-row energy. Controlled factors instead leave 93.8–96.1% of final expert-state delta energy in the output-null space, with negligible padding projection (0.06–0.19%).
- Executable alignment strengthens later in the trajectory. From step 50 to 100, paraphrase executed-row energy rises from 2.56% to 6.93%, main-view from 3.55% to 6.20%, and wrist-view from 3.91% to 8.00%. Proposal padding burden falls from 65.5% to 49.0% while action dispersion triples. This supports a time-resolved transition from generic denoising/context computation toward executable decision geometry.
- Direction-selective gain also confirms across states: median action/VLM displacement gain is 35.0 for alternate goal, 12.6 for paraphrase, and only 2.40/2.65 for main/wrist mean replacement. The key signal is direction plus decoder coupling, not representation amplitude.
- Novelty search found a close 2026 neighbor, COAST, which learns success/failure conceptor subspaces and steers residual activations across flow, autoregressive, and diffusion policies, explicitly framing an action-expert decoding bottleneck. Any claim that “latent knowledge is bottlenecked before action” would overlap.
- The current differentiator is narrower: analytically decompose policy-local changes using the actual executable decoder/Jacobian into executed-output, padding/auxiliary-output, and output-null components, then combine that geometry with matched-state recoverability and self-specificity. The initial search found no VLA paper performing this decomposition, but output-potent/output-null analysis is established in motor neuroscience and cannot itself be claimed as a new mathematical idea.
- Recent action-head redesign and direct-grounding-injection papers independently reinforce that the action interface is a bottleneck, but they optimize the head rather than use its local row/null geometry to audit whether predictive hidden information is executable, reflective, or implementation-only.
- COAST's appendix exposes the exact methodological gap relevant here: it mean-pools all action-horizon tokens for pi0.5 and all 49 state/future/action tokens for GR00T before fitting outcome subspaces. It preserves denoising time in one variant, but its labels come from separate rollouts and it neither matches physical state nor fixes proposal noise. Consequently, its success/failure subspace may still mix common state difficulty, policy competence, proposal luck, time, and token role.
- A July 2026 VLA paper uses a Jacobian null-space argument to explain why weight-space regularization can miss output collapse during fine-tuning. That is not the same object as this project's inference-time hidden-to-executed-action row/null decomposition, but it rules out claiming generic “Jacobian null spaces for VLAs” as novel.
- The narrowed candidate contribution is therefore **Executable–Reflective Subspace Decomposition (ERSD)** inside the broader CPRD design: at every action token and denoising time, split a hidden displacement into (1) an executed-effect-potent component, (2) an auxiliary/padding-output component, and (3) an output-null component; then ask whether each component predicts same-state recoverability `V`, proposal quality `Q`, and policy-specific limitations under controlled factor interventions.
- ERSD makes a falsifiable “knowledge–action gap” claim only if output-null features predict the model's *own* residual recoverability after controlling identical physical state, full proposed plan/effect, noise, and other policies—while matched row-space interventions alter the predicted action/effect and matched continuation outcome. Null-space decodability alone remains correlational context storage.
- **Matched physical state is not sufficient in LIBERO.** The official `regenerate_obs_from_state` restores only flattened MuJoCo state, while robosuite control carries hidden state outside MuJoCo: interpolator starts/goals, accumulated gripper command, history buffers, episode clocks, observable timing, and RNG. These are causal parents of the next transition and must be included in any claimed same-state branch.
- The real contract test makes the effect large rather than theoretical. Replaying an identical three-action sequence after a MuJoCo-only restore changed final simulator state by `0.037207`, main/wrist pixels by up to `95/172` intensity levels, and the gripper accumulator from `[0.25,-0.25]` to `[-0.50,0.50]`. Restoring the full runtime state yielded bit-identical observations and `7.12e-15` maximum simulator difference.
- This creates a required **computational-state certificate** for CRD/CPRD: a branch point is admissible only if repeated action probes after restoration reproduce physics, both images, predicates, controller/gripper state, and termination bookkeeping within predeclared tolerances. Papers using simulator snapshots without testing this stronger contract can unintentionally attribute controller-memory leakage to policy stochasticity or latent competence.
- The explicit language-conflict control reveals a **semantic-routing reversal**. The instruction negates the original drawer goal and ends with the same feasible cabinet goal used by the clean alternate condition. Layer-15 VLM geometry is weakly but consistently closer to the alternate at both steps (`+0.067` to `+0.073` normalized alignment), so the semantic distinction is not simply absent from the backbone.
- At step 50, the action path reverses that VLM tendency: layer-15 expert alignment moves from `+0.0366` at denoising start to `-0.1907` at the final call, executed velocity is original-aligned throughout, and the final action alignment is `-0.31` median. At step 100, the expert and executed velocity instead become increasingly alternate-aligned, ending at `+0.255` and `+0.330`, with final action alignment `+0.328` median.
- This supports a sharper hypothesis than “language prior too strong/weak”: **the flow decoder conditionally gates or rotates an available semantic direction according to scene/trajectory state**. The same language representation can be overridden by a visually implied action template early and used later. Because the saved counterfactual state was reached under the original task, the result does not yet distinguish harmful trajectory-template capture from rational off-policy recovery; matched computational-state branches from shared pre-divergence states are required.
- The contradiction result also shows why mean-pooled latent similarity can miss the mechanism: its pooled action alignment is near zero only because two strong, opposite step-conditioned effects cancel. Language grounding must be evaluated by event × denoising-time × executable-subspace interactions, not one task-level delta.
- The official training state/action audit does not support literal horizon-50 demonstration copying for the four Phase 2 states. Across 190,508 train windows there are zero exact matches; proposal-to-same-task nearest RMSE has median `0.553`, compared with training self-nearest median `0.449`, and the median proposal sits at the 80th percentile of ordinary training-window redundancy.
- Action-only nearest-neighbour retrieval produces a serious false positive: other-task candidates appear closer (`0.446`) than same-task candidates, but have median standardized robot-state mismatch `1.707` and are dominated by low-motion, saturated-gripper tails. Memorisation audits must jointly match state, geometry, task/event, and motion; action DTW alone can confuse generic stopping behavior with retrieval.
- State-conditioning exposes another negative result. Among the 128 closest same-task robot states, nearest action RMSE worsens from `0.553` to `0.813` (a `1.471×` penalty). The policy proposals are not simply the continuation of the closest proprioceptive training state, although the available state omits object geometry.
- A narrower trajectory-template hypothesis survives. Same-seed plans at step-matched success/failure states differ by `0.222`, close to within-state proposal dispersion `0.176`, whereas different-landmark plans differ by about `0.624`. This is compatible with a phase-locked policy attractor or skill prototype, but not proof: controlled object-milestone, off-path recovery, layout-shift, and replanning-horizon interventions are required.
- The complete Phase 2 synthesis is `docs/phase2_discoveries_and_ersd.md`. Its central cross-architecture object is an environment-effect Jacobian, not a raw latent alignment: each model-local displacement is split into executable, auxiliary, and output-null components and tested against matched-state `V`, proposal deviation `Q-V`, policy residual competence, and factor interactions.
- CPRD self-knowledge must be **self-specific**. A hidden representation should improve grouped held-out prediction of its own policy-state residual or proposal deviation more than matched outcomes of other policies after privileged state, pixels, history, full proposal effect, noise, and retrieval distance are controlled. Predicting every policy equally is common scene-difficulty awareness, not introspective competence.
- Phase 2 is complete but remains at fixed-observation and retrieval evidence. The next causal discriminator is a computational-state-certified branched smoke study; π0.5/GR00T downloads are intentionally deferred until the SmolVLA design survives this cheaper gate.

## Phase 3 Design Findings

- The missing historical full-runtime state can be recovered without a canonical policy rerun: reset the same LIBERO task/initial-state index, replay the immutable episode's recorded environment-facing actions, validate robot state and both cameras at each selected landmark against the archived observation, then capture the complete Phase 2 snapshot.
- A Phase 3 state is admissible only if three checks pass: archive replay fidelity, immediate full-state round trip, and a repeated short probe-action certificate. A successful in-memory restore alone is not sufficient evidence that the reconstructed state matches the original trajectory.
- Branch data must distinguish source trajectory seed, proposal noise seed, and continuation schedule seed. Reusing one ambiguous `seed` column would invalidate `V`, `Q`, common-random-number pairing, and resume deduplication.
- The ten-state ceiling should be allocated across available risk sets rather than forcing nonexistent task-4 step-100 states. A defensible smoke bank is five task-3 states spanning both steps 50 and 100 plus five task-4 step-50 states. With two goal instructions, four proposal seeds, and two continuation schedules, this is exactly the approved 160-branch ceiling.
- Because task 4 terminates before step 100 for every archived seed, continuing it past success would change the scientific estimand and risk vector-environment autoreset contamination. Phase 3 must stop at native or common-predicate success and report landmark coverage explicitly.
- The two shared-scene goals reduce to portable LIBERO predicates over identical named entities: drawer goal `In(akita_black_bowl_1, wooden_cabinet_1_top_region)` and cabinet goal `On(akita_black_bowl_1, wooden_cabinet_1_top_side)`. The native task-3 BDDL does not separately require `Open`; its goal is the bowl-inside predicate, so the common evaluator must not silently add a stricter success definition.
- Both BDDL files define the same fixtures, movable objects, and initial placement regions. Cross-goal evaluation can therefore use each environment's live `object_states_dict` and LIBERO predicate registry without assuming that native `check_success()` represents the counterfactual instruction.
- LeRobot's LIBERO wrapper increments `init_state_id` on every reset, independent of the supplied random seed. Reconstructing episode `i` therefore requires a fresh task environment with `episode_index=i` (or an explicitly restored `init_state_id`), not repeated `reset(seed=i)` calls on one environment.
- The vector environment uses same-step autoreset. Branch execution must inspect `final_info` and stop before consuming the automatically reset observation; otherwise one branch can silently continue in a different initial state and corrupt outcome, object geometry, and state certificates.
- A deeper wrapper audit reveals a provenance defect in the completed benchmark metadata: LeRobot's scalar `LiberoEnv.step()` calls `reset()` itself on success, and the enclosing `SyncVectorEnv` is also configured for same-step autoreset. The rollout loop then explicitly resets before the next episode. Successful episodes can therefore advance `init_state_id` multiple times, so the recorded `initial_state_id=episode_index` is not necessarily the state actually observed.
- Gymnasium source confirms the second reset: `SyncVectorEnv.step()` in `SAME_STEP` mode calls the scalar environment's `reset()` whenever the scalar step returns terminated/truncated. Because LeRobot has already reset inside that scalar step, each success consumes at least two unseen init states before the experiment loop's next explicit reset.
- Phase 3 must resolve each source episode's true LIBERO init-state index by matching its archived step-0 cameras and robot state against candidate resets. It must store the resolved index and fidelity, not trust the historical metadata. This is both a state-bank requirement and a durable warning for all prior seed-matched intervention interpretations.
- Archive evidence confirms material mismatch. Comparing step-0 `policy_state` for the same nominal episode ID gives exact equality in only `60/90` clean–paraphrase pairs, `33/90` clean–blur pairs, and `14/90` clean–wrist-mask pairs. Mismatches begin at episode indices 1–2 and reach maximum state differences around `0.01–0.025` in the sampled output.
- The earlier 90-episode intervention success differences must therefore be downgraded from matched-initial-state causal effects to condition-level run contrasts, except on the explicitly verified identical-state subset. Outcome-dependent reset consumption and possibly trajectory-dependent RNG history contaminate the nominal seed pairing. A corrected matched-subset reanalysis is required before reusing those causal claims.
- Phase 3's principal engineering advance is consequently stronger than state restoration alone: each branch originates from one in-memory certified snapshot, and all factor/proposal/continuation cells restore that same object rather than recreate a state from a nominal seed.
- Archive replay fidelity can be checked without invoking the model: raw reset/step cameras transpose directly to the saved `3x256x256` uint8 arrays, and the saved 7-D joint state is extracted from the same raw observation. This gives a strict, inexpensive state-source certificate before any branch GPU use.
- Environment image resolution is part of state provenance. The canonical benchmark stores 256×256 cameras, while the current relocated generic runtime helper asks LIBERO for 360×360. Phase 3 must use 256×256 for archive replay and branching so rendering, policy inputs, and archive-fidelity checks are aligned.
- Efficient branch factorization is possible without changing the estimand. Compute and capture each first proposal once per `state × goal-instruction × proposal-seed` (80 exact queries), store its environment-facing 50-action chunk, and replay that same chunk into both continuation schedules. Re-querying the identical first proposal for each continuation would waste compute and introduce an avoidable fidelity risk.
- After the fixed first chunk, continuation replans can use `predict_action_chunk` with explicit deterministic noise seeds; no continuation hidden capture is required for the Phase 3 primary targets. Reset the policy before every branch so observation queues cannot leak between branches, and execute chunks directly so action-queue bookkeeping is not conflated with environment restoration.
- Phase 2 has an input-contract bug: the rollout saves camera arrays before `LiberoProcessorStep`, which rotates both image axes by 180°, but `run_phase2_forward_gate.py` rebuilds the model batch directly from those unflipped arrays.
